# I. Executive Summary

The Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of 1998, codified at 28 U.S.C. §§
651--658, mandates that each United States district court authorize the
use of alternative dispute resolution (ADR) processes in civil cases.
The Act promotes settlement efficiency, reduces litigation burdens, and
reinforces judicial economy by integrating structured non-judicial
conflict resolution options.

# II. Legal Mandate and Scope

Under §651, every federal district court must implement a local ADR
program and provide litigants with access to at least one ADR process,
typically mediation, early neutral evaluation (ENE), or arbitration. The
statute applies to most civil actions except habeas corpus or actions in
which jurisdiction is vested exclusively in a federal tribunal.

# III. Functional Objectives of ADR under the Act

1\. Increase access to justice by providing time-efficient and
cost-effective resolution mechanisms.\
2. Reduce court congestion by resolving matters pre-trial.\
3. Foster collaborative, non-adversarial outcomes that may preserve
ongoing relationships between parties.\
4. Support judicial discretion to compel participation in ADR where
appropriate.

# IV. Integration with District of Arizona Procedures

The District of Arizona complies with the ADR Act through Local Rule
LRCiv 16.2, which governs mediation and judicial settlement conferences.
Judges may refer cases to ADR sua sponte or upon motion. Litigants are
expected to comply with scheduling orders directing ADR involvement, and
confidentiality is protected under local enforcement of §652(d).

# V. Procedural Framework

\- Initial Case Management: ADR evaluation discussed at Rule 26(f)
conference.\
- Scheduling Order: Judges may designate deadlines for ADR referral.\
- Neutral Appointment: Parties may agree or court may assign certified
mediators or evaluators.\
- Reporting and Confidentiality: Outcomes reported under §651(b), with
confidentiality protected.

# VI. Ethical and Regulatory Implications

\- Confidentiality: Neutrals and parties are bound by strict
confidentiality rules.\
- Good Faith Participation: While not always mandatory, courts may
enforce participation where ordered.\
- Pro Se Considerations: The ADR framework must accommodate
unrepresented parties through simplified protocols and clear notice.

### **Functional Description: Complaint Drafting (FRCP 8 & 10 Compliance)**

**1.0 Purpose**  
This Functional Description (FD) provides a generic guide for drafting a federal complaint that complies with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP), specifically **Rule 8** and **Rule 10**. Its primary purpose is to prevent the drafting of a "shotgun pleading," which is subject to dismissal. The principles herein are derived from established court orders and binding precedent.  
**2.0 The "Shotgun Pleading" Prohibition (Violations of FRCP 8\)**  
A complaint that fails to provide a "short and plain statement of the claim" (FRCP 8(a)(2)) or is not "simple, concise, and direct" (FRCP 8(d)(1)) is deemed a "shotgun pleading" and will be dismissed.  
The following structures are **expressly prohibited**:

* **Improper Incorporation:** A complaint must not "incorporate every antecedent allegation by reference into each subsequent claim for relief".  
* **Scattered Factual Elements:** A complaint may be dismissed where the factual elements of a cause of action are scattered throughout the document rather than being organized into a short and plain statement of the claim.  
* **Separate "Factual Allegations" Section:** The complaint must not use a lengthy, separate "Factual Allegations" section and then "incorporate by reference" select paragraphs into 27 separate "Causes of Action".  
* **Burden on the Court:** This practice is forbidden because the Court is not required to "comb through" or "parse the complaint searching for allegations". As one court noted, "judges are not like pigs, hunting for truffles buried in briefs".

**3.0 Mandatory Pleading Structure (The 5-Point Standard)**  
To comply with FRCP 8 and 10, every complaint must adhere to the following structure.

### **3.1 One Claim Per Count**

* The complaint must include **only one legal claim per count**.  
* Asserting **multiple legal claims** against **multiple defendants** within a single count is a violation of FRCP 10(b) and constitutes a shotgun pleading.

### **3.2 Self-Contained Claims**

* Facts supporting a particular claim must be discussed **in the section devoted to that claim**.  
* For **each** Cause of Action (Count), and for **each** Defendant named in that Count, the pleading *must* state:  
  1. The specific law the Plaintiff believes was violated;  
  2. The name of the single Defendant who violated that law;  
  3. **Exactly** what that Defendant did or failed to do;  
  4. How the action or inaction of that specific Defendant is connected to the violation of the law; and  
  5. What **specific injury** the Plaintiff suffered because of that Defendant's conduct.  
* **Affirmative Linkage:** A Plaintiff *must* affirmatively link the conduct of *each* named Defendant with the specific injury suffered. Failure to do so will result in the allegations against that Defendant being dismissed.  
* **Conclusory Allegations:** "Conclusory allegations" that a Defendant or group of Defendants violated a right are not acceptable and will be dismissed.  
* **"Doe" Defendants:** If the names of individual Defendants are unknown, they must be listed as "John (or Jane) Doe 1, John Doe 2," etc.. The body of the complaint must allege facts supporting how *each particular Doe Defendant* violated the Plaintiff's rights. It is insufficient to list a category (e.g., "John Does 1-10") and make conclusory allegations against them as a group.

**4.0 Rules for Amended Complaints**  
If a complaint is dismissed with leave to amend, the following rules are mandatory:

* **Supersedes Original:** An amended complaint **supersedes** the original complaint in its entirety.  
* **Treated as Nonexistent:** After amendment, the Court will treat the original complaint as "nonexistent".  
* **Must Be Standalone:** The amended complaint must be retyped or rewritten in its entirety.  
* **No Incorporation:** The amended complaint **may not incorporate any part** of the original or prior amended complaints by reference.  
* **Waiver of Claims:** Any cause of action that was raised in the original complaint but is *not* alleged in the amended complaint is **waived**.

**5.0 Key Jurisdictional Limitations**

### **5.1 Sovereign Immunity (Eleventh Amendment)**

* Under the Eleventh Amendment, a state or state agency **may not be sued in federal court without its consent**.  
* The supplemental jurisdiction statute (28 U.S.C. § 1367\) does *not* authorize federal courts to exercise jurisdiction over state law claims against nonconsenting states.

### **5.2 Section 1983 Limitations**

* A state is **not a "person"** for the purposes of a 42 U.S.C. § 1983 claim.

### **5.3 Incorporation of Other Cases**

* A Plaintiff **may not incorporate, by reference or otherwise, claims they have raised in other pending or previously litigated cases**.  
* An *in forma pauperis* complaint that repeats pending or previously litigated claims may be considered "abusive" and dismissed under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e).

**6.0 Appendix: Case Law and Legal Principles**  
This section details the legal principles derived from the case law cited in the guiding Court Order.

* **Bailey v. Johnson, 846 F.2d 1019 (5th Cir. 1988):** Supports the principle that repeating previously litigated claims may be considered abusive and subject to dismissal under § 1915(e).  
* **Cato v. United States, 70 F.3d 1103 (9th Cir. 1995):** An *in forma pauperis* complaint that repeats pending or previously litigated claims may be considered abusive and dismissed under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e).  
* **Davis v. Coca-Cola Bottling Co. Consol., 516 F.3d 955 (11th Cir. 2008):** A complaint alleging numerous forms of Title VII violations against multiple defendants in a single count violated Rule 10(b) and constituted a shotgun pleading.  
* **De Beers Consol. Mines v. United States, 325 U.S. 212 (1945):** Preliminary injunctive relief is appropriate for "intermediate relief of the same character as that which may be granted finally," but is not proper for matters lying "wholly outside the issues in the suit".  
* **Destfino v. Kennedy, 2009 WL 63566 (E.D. Cal. 2009):** Harshly criticizes incorporating each preceding paragraph, regardless of relevancy, as a "shotgun pleading" that violates Rule 8 and interferes with the court's ability to administer justice.  
* **Destfino v. Reiswig, 630 F.3d 952 (9th Cir. 2011):** Affirmed the district court's ruling in Destfino v. Kennedy.  
* **Devose v. Herrington, 42 F.3d 470 (8th Cir. 1994):** To obtain injunctive relief, a party "must necessarily establish a relationship between the injury claimed in the party's motion and the conduct asserted in the complaint".  
* **Ferdik v. Bonzelet, 963 F.2d 1258 (9th Cir. 1992):** An amended complaint supersedes the original complaint, which is thereafter treated as nonexistent. Failure to comply with a court order to amend may result in dismissal.  
* **Ferrell v. Durbin, 311 F. App'x 253 (11th Cir. 2009):** Neither the Court of Appeals nor the district court is "required to parse the complaint searching for allegations" that form the basis of a plaintiff's claims.  
* **Gilbreath v. Cutter Biological, Inc., 931 F.2d 1320 (9th Cir. 1991):** A state is not a "person" for purposes of § 1983\.  
* **Hal Roach Studios, Inc. v. Richard Feiner & Co., 896 F.2d 1542 (9th Cir. 1989):** An amended complaint supersedes the original.  
* **Indep. Towers of Washington v. Washington, 350 F.3d 925 (9th Cir. 2003):** Articulates the principle that "judges are not like pigs, hunting for truffles buried in briefs".  
* **Lacey v. Maricopa Cnty., 693 F.3d 896 (9th Cir. 2012):** Any cause of action raised in an original complaint but not re-alleged in a subsequent amended complaint is waived.  
* **Lopez v. Smith, 203 F.3d 1122 (9th Cir. 2000):** If a pleading *can* be cured by the allegation of other facts, a pro se litigant is entitled to an opportunity to amend before dismissal.  
* **McHenry v. Renne, 84 F.3d 1172 (9th Cir. 1996):** Supports the dismissal of a complaint for failure to satisfy Rule 8(a).  
* **Miss Universe, Inc. v. Flesher, 605 F.2d 1130 (9th Cir. 1979):** The decision to grant or deny a preliminary injunction is within the Court's discretion.  
* **Pennhurst State Sch. & Hosp. v. Halderman, 465 U.S. 89 (1984):** Under the Eleventh Amendment, a state or state agency may not be sued in federal court without its consent.  
* **Raygor v. Regents of Univ. of Minnesota, 534 U.S. 533 (2002):** The supplemental jurisdiction statute does not authorize federal district courts to exercise jurisdiction over state law claims against nonconsenting States.  
* **Rizzo v. Goode, 423 U.S. 362 (1976):** Supports the requirement for a plaintiff to plead specific facts linking a specific defendant's conduct to a specific injury.  
* **Sparling v. Hoffman Const. Co., 864 F.2d 635 (9th Cir. 1988):** A complaint with factual elements scattered throughout, rather than organized into a "short and plain statement," may be dismissed for failure to satisfy Rule 8(a).  
* **United States v. Dunkel, 927 F.2d 955 (7th Cir. 1991):** The origin of the "pigs, hunting for truffles" quotation.  
* **Wagner v. First Horizon Pharm. Corp., 464 F.3d 1273 (11th Cir. 2006):** Defines "shotgun pleadings" as those that "incorporate every antecedent allegation by reference into each subsequent claim for relief" and notes they "wreak havoc on the judicial system".

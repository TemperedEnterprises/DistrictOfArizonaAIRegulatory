# **Latin Legal Concepts**

These terms describe fundamental legal ideas, states, or constructs that
are not strictly procedures, contracts, or property terms.

-   **Actus Reus:** \"Guilty act.\" The physical act or omission that
    > constitutes a crime (primarily in criminal law).

-   **Alter Ego:** \"Another self.\" A distinct personality, often used
    > in corporate law when a corporation\'s separate identity is
    > disregarded because it\'s merely an extension of an individual.

-   **Animus Nocendi:** \"Intention to harm.\" The subjective state of
    > mind of the author of a crime, with reference to the exact
    > knowledge of illegal content of his behavior.

-   **Bona Vacantia:** \"Vacant goods.\" Ownerless goods or property,
    > which typically passes to the Crown or state.

-   **Casus Belli:** \"An act or event that provokes a war.\" A
    > justification for acts of war.

-   **Corpus Delicti:** \"Body of the crime.\" The facts proving that a
    > crime has been committed; does not refer to the victim\'s body.

-   **Contra Bonos Mores:** \"Against good morals.\" Conduct considered
    > contrary to community standards of morality.

-   **In Flagrante Delicto:** \"In blazing offense.\" Caught in the act
    > of committing a crime.

-   **Mens Rea:** \"Guilty mind.\" The mental state or intent of the
    > defendant at the time the crime was committed (primarily in
    > criminal law, but the concept of intent applies in intentional
    > torts).

-   **Quasi:** \"As if\" or \"resembling.\" Used to describe something
    > that is not truly something else, but has similar properties
    > (e.g., quasi-contract, quasi-judicial).

-   **Res Gestae:** \"Things done.\" Spontaneous statements made during
    > or immediately after an event, admissible as evidence.

-   **Vis Major:** \"Superior force.\" An irresistible natural event
    > (like a flood or earthquake) preventing performance of a contract
    > or excusing liability; often synonymous with Act of God.
